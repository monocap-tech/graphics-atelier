# G06-F06 — Illustrated motion: cutout, parameterized deformation, skeletal rigging and actual lineage fragments

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status: foundational source-led R1, with official tool diagrams/readable examples; NO new rig, model, project, preview run, art procurement or audience test.** Continues [F01–F04 manga](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-04.md) and [F05 drawn animation](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-05.md), while bridging [G02-P01 illustration procurement and adaptation](studies/G02-P01.md) and [G03 multipart art](G00-A04.md) where extant. The point is **what the artist makes editable**, not a new tool recommendation or a presumption that rigging replaces drawing. [STD01](../docs/EFFECTS_STANDARD.md) governs eventual production testing, not this research sequence.

## Foundational question

How can the **look of a particular authored illustration** be retained while making its parts and expressions move? What must be newly drawn, split, extended, modeled, re-rigged or replaced? How does this differ from drawn animation's sequence of genga, douga, exposure and compositing, and what actual dated production accounts show transfer across media?

The term *2D rigging* covers substantially different practices. A deforming drawn portrait, a pivoted sprite cutout, a mesh skinned to bones, and a frame-drawn effect over a rigged figure are **not the same method**. The output may look continuous but is not necessarily a 3D model, physically faithful articulation or an independent effects layer. Manga panel construction remains different from animation time; likeness and source art rights persist throughout.

## What changes from F05: authored variable vs authored drawing

| Method (examples, not exclusive categories) | What is reusable | What is authored per motion | Typical extra burden / failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame drawing (F05: genga/douga) | Drafts, pose references, model sheets, timesheet structures, effects elements | New contours/intermediate forms/exposures and camera/compositing directions | Many revisions across drawings; inconsistency between frames if unchecked; **not** automatically less expressive |
| Pivoted cutout / hierarchical sprite (early SpriteStudio and many independent tools) | Drawn body pieces, anchors, transforms, reusable poses | Pivot rotations/translations, ordering, visibility, keyframes | Joint seams, missing underdraw, unnatural foreshortening or depth swap |
| Parameterized illustrated mesh (Cubism/E-mote) | Colored art, separated layers, ArtMeshes, deformers, keyed expressions, blended motions | Draw missing/hidden regions, mesh/deformer/keyform and masking; expression-specific replacement parts as needed | Distorted linework, shrinking on naive interpolation, occlusion splits and nontrivial initial art/rig labor; rotations cannot synthesize unseen anatomy for free |
| Skeletal mesh/weights (Spine/SpriteStudio ≥6) | Bones, attachments, animation curves, skin changes, mesh weights | Limb/hair/cloth weighting, deformed shapes, replacement art, corrective images, timelines | Mesh folding, undesirable stretch/edges, incorrect pivot/weight blending, runtime constraints |
| Hybrid with composited/drawn effects | Above + drawings, photographic transforms and particle emitters | Distinct authoring of where glow/smoke/mark exists, when, and how it travels with subject | Compositing/color/depth conflict, event timing, scene overload; which owns the effect can vary |

### The *source image* is not equal to the model

Cubism explicitly separates the artist's highly editable **material separation PSD**, the merged **import PSD**, and model/ArtMesh/deformer/animation data. The **runtime artifact** is another object. Art must be drawn behind movable parts; art and rig may require iteration after viewing the movement. This matches the G02-P01 concern about custody through source → transformation → runtime, but F06 **does not** assert G02's proposed proportional numbers or asset contracts are validated.

In art terms, an arm painted beautifully in one view may not survive arbitrary rotation. A bangs highlight that looks correct when static can look pasted-on when the mesh bends. Rigging doesn't 'solve' drawing, it **places constraints on where drawing has to anticipate a range of movement**. A hand-drawn corrective or swapped illustration can still be superior for strongly changed pose or perspective. No quantitative visual comparison was performed here.

## Three concrete relations to the ongoing effects investigation

1. **Emotion is designed as coordinated illustration behavior.** In *Project SEKAI*, scenario, expression assets, modelers, animators and scripting collaborate; overlapping eye motions (automatic vs intentional) need coordinated runtime events. 'Psychological effect' is partly authored in the face and acting, not only a separate particle emitter or glow. Distinguish designer intent from response.
2. **Restraint is part of direction, not merely performance.** M2's *Witch's Garden* makers explicitly report choosing **not** to keep every portrait constantly breathing or moving so the speaking character remains the focus, and to reduce compute. Demonstrates interplay between scene attention and runtime cost, not a universally right idle animation policy.
3. **Effect assets and rigged art can share an authoring timeline without being genealogically one thing.** SpriteStudio's **2015 particle module** followed its existing hierarchical sprite editor, while **2017 mesh bones** were a later addition. That sequence refutes any implication that a contemporary features list represents the original historical tool or that all effects began as skeletal deformations.

## Documented genealogical pieces, and what is not proven

- **M2:** tool developer's direct trace **multi-joint game motion editor (around 2009 named games) → 2012–13 illustrated standee E-mote (Witch's Garden) → specialized designer-facing Lite interface**. A strong named institutional genealogy *within game development* but no independent repository/code inspection.
- **OPTPiX:** developer history c.2002 sprite tool idea, reworked 2013 cross-platform animator → 2015 particles added → 2017 bones/masks → 2018 direct deformer. A documented **product-feature chronology**, not the ancestry of techniques in manga/anime or an audit of which titles used each feature.
- **Live2D:** its CEO reports independent graphics experimentation around 2000, ambitions to preserve authored 2D line quality and later adoption through game-industry partners. This is a developer's innovation narrative, not an established origin from 19th/20th century 2D cutout film or manga. A 2024 game pipeline is direct operational evidence of contemporary reuse rather than ancient ancestry.
- **Cross-media migration remains open:** possible predecessors such as paper cutout, multiplane/rostrum-camera, TV motion graphics, Flash and puppet animation must be researched as independent material/organizational histories. The existence of analog sliding photography in Ozawa's F05 smoke example establishes a **similar operational relation**, not direct influence on a 2010s rigging platform. Likewise an animated manga manpu in a game is not proof its technical author learned from manga convention rather than a general visual tradition.

## Limitations and next bounded inquiry

All evidence is **official developer documentation, public interviews or illustrated tutorial**. No installed Live2D, E-mote, Spine or SpriteStudio ran; no PSD, rig model, exported JSON/SDK binary or commercial cut inspected; no source art rights approved for reuse. Live2D manual pages span generations; vendor license and capabilities are **version-specific**. Do not choose software, infer user proficiency or impose an editor merely because it appears in documentation. No audience test, 'smaller rig always better' benchmark, professional-level result, complete system genealogy or confirmation of historic manga→anime→game methods.
