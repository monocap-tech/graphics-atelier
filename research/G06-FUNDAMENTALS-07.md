# G06-F07 — Traceable crossings among illustration, animation, film technology and game graphics

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status: bounded source-led historical investigation (R1 for named testimonies and institutional archival descriptions); no original production, viewer experiment or complete technical genealogy.** Continues [F01–F04 manga](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-04.md), [F05 animation](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-05.md), [F06 illustrated rigging](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-06.md). This is an answer to the question **whether apparent visual kinship demonstrates a historical production lineage**. It does not automatically. No software trial, commissioned art, game assets extracted, full film read, audience response measurement or prototype.

## Method: do not use a single manga → anime → games arrow

For each claimed crossing, specify **carrier** (artist/personnel, tool, drawing/film material, teaching/document, particular artwork, engine/hardware, or only a formal resemblance), **source/destination** (and date), **attested operation**, and **evidence status**. These are **edge types, not achievement levels**:

- **Visual inspiration:** a maker names a reference (e.g., film/anime/illustration), not an inherited authoring method.
- **Production-method transfer:** a maker explicitly applies or adapts one production procedure to another medium; note what changed.
- **Tool lineage:** named developers show a tool was adapted from a prior tool/work; distinguish same software from same artistic output.
- **Personnel/training transfer:** specific practitioner moves and describes using acquired expertise; a hiring policy alone documents openness, not completed transfer.
- **Technical parallel or historical hypothesis:** comparable operations or a scholar's plausible chain without direct personnel/tool custody; **not** proof of descent.
- **Independent hardware/production constraint:** a medium-specific invention or design choice that explains surface likeness/difference without assuming borrowing.

No equation between cutout and modern skeletal weights, animation's photographic slide and arcade sprite scaling, or hand-drawn FX and game particles without a dated source establishing actual transfer.

## What this changes in the atelier

The foundation is **not** a family tree with a single trunk. It is a network with at least three distinct kinds of evidence:

1. A **visual reference** can be transferred without a technician or an algorithm (Suzuki's anime, cinema and illustration references).
2. **Production operations and tools** can be repurposed without inherited image style (M2's multijoint action-game editor turned illustrated-portrait tool).
3. **Aesthetic production principles** can be reconstructed independently with different technology (Arc System Works self-studied anime and explicitly migrated its own 2D animation decisions into real-time 3D), and only later be strengthened by direct staff migration.

In contrast, the cutout/slide archival history shows **an older independent mechanical repertoire**. Its relevance to present-day compositing/rigging can be *operational* without yielding a demonstrable named personnel lineage into arcade hardware. F05's Ozawa split/slide smoke drawing is a separate modern animator instruction grounded in similar drawing-versus-photography distinction, not proof an arcade sprite scaler was derived from that animator or optical table.

### Especially important for the Space Harrier/AM2 application

The game is a meeting of *artistic references*, a *hardware-defined representation* and *playable sensory presentation*. It is **not** simply an animated manga or anime effect. Suzuki says visual impact demanded a comparatively large protagonist, and memory limits changed jet to human and realistic geometry to unusual scenery. His 1986 remarks emphasize color, gradient/pastel treatment and cabinet/sound/graphics immersion. This supports the user’s broader original premise: a game-graphics production lineage must inspect the **display machine, game decisions and artwork together**. It does **not** provide pixel-perfect exported sprite contracts or actual renderer code. A faithful reproduction cannot be inferred from screenshots or Japanese graphic conventions alone.
