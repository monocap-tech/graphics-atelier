# G06-F01 — Fundamentals: page construction before isolated effects

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status:** first bounded source-led foundation pass (R1 for qualified descriptions; not R3, not a historical lineage proof). This is a return to source practice after STD01/BR01 and the proposed VIGIL vignette. **No prototype, new tool standard, viewer test or production target was selected.** The next step is to understand the page and artist's work, not to manufacture another isolated sign. This inquiry can coexist with G06-STD01's production-capacity objective.

## Question and starting assumptions

How does an artist make expressive and material qualities operative **within a manga page**: its panels, figures, background, tonal surfaces, marks and lettering? We do not assume that manga gave rise to all anime or Japanese game effects, that every graphic treatment has a code-like meaning, or that manga's production operations translate to live games unchanged. Starting with manga is a **research choice**, not a proven genealogical origin.

## Foundational distinctions, not a new taxonomy

- **Page / composition precedes effect isolation in analysis.** ネーム, コマ割り, figures, whitespace, lettering, vantage and page sequence affect what any drawn treatment does. A concentrated streak can be a background operation and a way to reorganize attention.
- **Representational material and expressive device are not exclusive.** A tone may render fabric, shadow, surface relief, mood or an entire shape; the same concrete technique differs by context and application.
- **Meaning, forcefulness and visual guidance are nonidentical.** A speed-line treatment may describe literal movement or energize a moment; emphasis can change perceived force without altering an underlying event.
- **Separability is not guaranteed.** Some effects sit beside the depicted subject, some overlay it, and some are inseparable from the subject's contour, deformation and environment; any pipeline of independent 'effect layers' is an engineering convenience only where appropriate.
- **Method and historical descent must remain separate.** Shogakukan supplies practical drawing procedure; Natsume and Sumiyama supply analytic frameworks; CELSYS supplies modern software tools. None alone proves that studio/game VFX personnel or techniques traveled from manga by a particular chain.

## Order of next observation (no prototype scheduled)

1. **Whole-page contextual study:** acquire lawful full-page or manuscript-sequence examples, distinguish panel arrangement and negative space before cropping individual marks. Prioritize Shogakukan's actual instructional sample and a documented analog tone/line manuscript where we can inspect stepwise stages; record attribution and medium.
2. **Material operations:** paper, ruler/nib/brush/white correction, ベタ, カケアミ, tone cut/rub/scrape/stack and digital equivalents, including registration/print/moire and labor. Do not collapse printed-dot technique and graphic content.
3. **Graphic conventions and reading:** 形喩, 漫符, 描き文字/音喩, gag shorthand, within-panel and between-panel sequences; ask which demands audience learning and which depend on spatial relations.
4. **Cross-medium comparison only thereafter:** source-led transition into animation effects drawing (原画, 動画, コマ打ち, 撮影, compositing), and separate investigation of illustrated cutout/rigging and 3D/particle/game FX. Personnel/institutional lineage needs dates, artists and evidence; similarity is insufficient.
5. **More physical/natural phenomena later:** observation and visual acting of smoke, water, fire, light, material processes, then game realization and production transfer when a substantive target is selected.

**Review condition:** after enough whole-page examples to explain how scene composition and marks function together, decide whether we need additional examples or can progress to material practice. No artificial quotas, effects lexicon, lab or programming until an actual open question makes it useful.
