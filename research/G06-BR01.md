## Effects at three simultaneously relevant levels

1. Representational/phenomenal: what appears, moves, changes, or is made perceptible (including nonexistent/abstract phenomena).
2. Situational/semantic: what relation, state, quality, dramatic situation or potential action is articulated, from whose perspective.
3. Experiential/psychological: what the treatment does to the spectator/player's attention, sensation, expectation, appraisal, rhythm, affect, enjoyment or felt participation.

These are analytical dimensions, not a historical taxonomy, exhaustive definition or one-way causal pipeline. A cue can be understood but unsatisfying; a scene can be moving without a proposition being decoded; a beautiful or autonomous phrase may have no mechanical referent. Meaning and felt experience can be intertwined. Neither intention nor visual fidelity nor regression correctness demonstrates audience response. Effects design is not exclusively psychological and many other crafts influence experience; no new disciplinary boundary or universal lexicon is asserted. Use the experiential dimension to choose and critique production, not create an unbounded new psychology program.

### Evidence and terminology

- SRC087 rechecked (Nintendo, https://www.nintendo.co.jp/jobs/introduction/design/work04.html): describes エフェクト via 効果/影響 and three broad roles (felt responsiveness/手ごたえ, intuitive comprehension, visual richness); deliberately designs nonexistent tactile responsiveness, exhilaration/accomplishment. States essence is designing and strengthening experience. Creator/role description, not controlled viewer study; actual movies not decoded. The colloquial/design rationale is supported, but why historical professional term 'special effects' was coined for precisely that reason is not established.
- SRC099 rechecked (4Gamer CEDEC 2011 https://www.4gamer.net/games/131/G013104/20110906084/): Hashimoto's reported recollection of Otomo wanting smoke vortices to express a character's worries in Steamboy, attention/memory via debris staging and deliberately irregular temporal accents; contrast target frame in a held impact vs displacement after a fast punch. Professional secondary reporting, not independent psychology results; no actual scenes timed or viewed. Important direct counterexample to limiting effects to a semantic label or physical replication.
- SRC199: [Ian Christie, The visible and the invisible: From 'tricks' to 'effects' (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17460654.2015.1036523). R0/limited abstract and search excerpt: history of trick work/stage machinery and shift toward dramatic expression in early cinema; full text publisher fetch 403. Not an established etymology of effects or evidence of a naming intention.
- SRC200: [SEGA 2025 training interview, CGWORLD](https://cgworld.jp/article/202505-cgkiso-2.html), 2025-05-29. R1 direct staff testimony: critique portfolios confined to assignments/elemental variants, black backgrounds that conceal failure in daytime, excessive spectacle obscuring a treasure chest's contents. Training exercises use cross texture sparkle and billboard/texture animated fire, then apply craft to enrich an empty chest, under drawing/runtime constraint. Intent and production criteria, not measured efficacy; no paid magazine/course examined. This is especially relevant as a bounded situated production exercise that unifies material, dramatic value, composition, craft and performance. It complements SRC102 (2019 SEGA TA fire→explosion→projectile→special attack with model/particle integration), rechecked full textual section; both are particular training examples, not universal curriculum.
- SRC201: [杉山大輝・井上裕人, アクションゲームにおける画面振動演出の単独効果と組み合わせ効果に関する基礎的検討 (DiGRA JAPAN summer proceedings, 2026, pp. 11–14)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/digrajprocsummer/2026/0/2026_11/_article/-char/en). R1 abstract only, J-STAGE release Oct 21, 2026. Paired-comparison impressions across five single/combined shake, slow and stop conditions; abstract reports combined conditions higher within 0.1–0.2 s and diminishing/masking changes at 0.4 s. Full methods, sample, task and statistics not audited; no durations adopted as presets or general optimum; no extrapolation to visual art or this project's audience.
- SRC202: [What Features Influence Impact Feel? A Study of Impact Feedback in Action Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06155) (2022 preprint/IEEE conference record). R1 abstract/search only; IEEE direct fetch 418; authors suggest hit-stop, sound coherence and camera control as important based on reported feature analysis; not causal proof or isolated visual effect experiment. Full text deferred absent target need.

See S07 Precure case for fictional relational participation distinct from computational input. It remains useful, not a requirement to reproduce. No full etymology research undertaken.
