# G06-F05 — Effects animation as drawn movement, exposure, and compositing

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status: source-led foundational R1 study plus directly inspected published instructional stills.** Continues [F01 page](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-01.md), [F02 panel](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-02.md), [F03 material](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-03.md), [F04 gag/graphic shorthand](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-04.md). No animation project, frame-by-frame decoded film, timed scene measurement, DCC/engine execution or viewer response. Effects animation is studied **as an independently attested craft**, not assumed to be manga graphics put in motion. No anime→game genealogy has been established. [G06-STD01](../docs/EFFECTS_STANDARD.md) is the later production evaluation standard, not an immediate prototype request.

## Question

What is an animated effect when its visible form may arise from (i) authored drawings, (ii) prescribed exposure and timing, (iii) movement or compositing of existing layers, (iv) special-effects finishing and (v) interaction with a character's acting, light, set and sound? Which parts of apparently one phenomenon are produced by which department, and how is their coordination authored? Do not conflate エフェクト作画 (effects drawing), 特殊効果 (finishing), 撮影 (compositing/photography), CG and digital screen effects; different works arrange responsibilities differently.

## Production/authoring distinctions

1. **絵コンテ, レイアウト and the cut's expressive design.** Anime's unit is a temporally unfolding *cut* within scene/film, not a page-panel. Storyboard and layout carry acting, spatial relationships, camera and anticipated mood. Compare F02 manga paneling by functional analogy only: a cut does not equal a panel and a cinema shot is not a visible two-page spread.
2. **原画 is authored motion, not just 'first/last sprite'.** Genga establish meaningful changes, staging and *how* a phenomenon transforms. Inbetween 動画 can provide continuity but can also fail if the key acting or shape development is wrong. Kyoto explicitly details cleanup as part of its dōga process for its named film.
3. **コマ打ち and タイムシート are distinct from unique drawing count.** Exposure controls **when/how long** a drawing is held on an output frame; two- or three-frame holds at a 24 fps reference occur in Japanese animation but are not a national invariant. Time sheet coordinates which numbered cell is visible and can record camera, dialogue/sound and compositing instructions. A three-key illustration plus intervening dōga drawings does not necessarily mean three output frames.
4. **Effect drawing manipulates the phenomenal form.** Flame silhouette can rise and extinguish, smoke interiors suggest rotation or depth, explosion debris can move on independent clocks. Rather than treating 'fire' as a preset, Ozawa's excerpts demonstrate contour/material planning, shorthand, keys, layering and anticipated screen result.
5. **The same animated appearance may be produced without many new drawings.** Ozawa's single smoke sheet divided into offset strips with SL arrows **delegates apparent motion to the compositing/photography process**. His other example layers A haze, B smoke, C flame and D burning subject, possibly with transmitted light and partial transparency. This is not proof of an exact modern rendering algorithm; it documents a production handoff and economy of authorship.
6. **Effects drawing ≠ special effects ≠ photography.** Toei's official company taxonomy calls 特殊効果 light/shadow/material treatment and 撮影 compositing; Kyoto's film making-of explicitly assigns several environmental phenomena to photography. Neither classification forbids animation effects drawing or CG, and in specific work duties can overlap. Infer who created a phenomenon only from source custody.
7. **The 'effect' is authored across departments.** The final effect of smoke, fire, weather or flash depends on timing, character interaction, visibility against background, color/tonal choices, shot scale and sound; some contributors enter at finishing, compositing or editing rather than the genga desk.
8. **Practical skills can cross drawing and CG without proven historical descent.** Inoue's printed teaching project is reported to serve younger and 3D staff (older SRC101/restricted), and the CEDEC talk directly joins anime and game practitioners. This supports a **possible transferable vocabulary and actual conversations**, but no unbroken chain from manga manpu into game VFX has been documented.

Visible irregular rising plume drawn in black with colored blue/yellow interior paths, red “SL” arrows on lower and middle sections. **Observed:** constituent bands and explicit displacement instructions. **Instructor's explanation:** only one original key drawing, separated into three moving components whose displacement is executed in photography. **Not observed:** actual exposure of each strip, movement curves, whole sequence, dialogue, character contact or timing-in-frames. Four annotated roles: A mist, B smoke, C fire, D burning object, with WXP translucency and indicated flame; specific selected layer boundaries visible. **Inference:** density/depth can be created by simultaneous layered roles and compositing, rather than contour proliferation alone. **Not measured:** composite export, alpha mathematics, time offsets, optical physicality or psychophysical depth.

## The actual fundamental bridge

Manga F01–F04 concerns **where marks sit, what the page withholds/reveals and what paper/reproduction enables**. Anime F05 adds **exposure, designed motion, number and relative timing of independent layers, compositing/photographic control, and audiovisual synchronization**. Not every drawn mark from manga becomes a drawn moving object in anime. Some anime effects can be produced in photography itself; conversely a single authored genga can serve as reusable material for composited movement.

The more useful question than “which particle effects originated in manga?” is: **what did each production tradition make editable, who had control of timing/space, and what did the artistic result rely on?** An exact human/institutional genealogy is a different, later evidence question.
