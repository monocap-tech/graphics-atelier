## A. Panoply: a panel is a scene camera, not a crop of a flat page

Erik Loyer's Unity library originated as an authoring tool for Upgrade Soul (creator README); repository now MIT-licensed according to metadata. The 2014–2023 reference manual describes independent 3D cameras, graphical grid-layout/keyframe authoring, 2D or 3D artwork, captions and audio; gesture and device tilt user input. Distinct artwork can occupy different depths and move independently. Manual warns panel cameras may see another panel's content unless separated spatially or via Unity layers. Manual pp.15–24 and 53–55, and renderer settings text inspected; screenshot request failed, so no original editor screenshot visually confirmed. No assumption that all legacy manual integrations (PlayMaker, render pipelines) correspond to current source.

Interpretation: a frame trajectory in screen space, scene projection and actor trajectory are independently authored. Global progression is dimensionless step, not simulation time; real time controls motion toward a chosen step. A panel can persist while its internal action or captions change. ## B. Japan-native mask authoring: OPTPiX SpriteStudio 6

Official 2017 mask tutorial (updated 2021) explicitly demonstrates cut-ins: instantiate a cut-in animation in a base animation, apply a mask part in the containing animation and exclude base artwork from the mask target. Alternative is to prepare reusable animation and clip it in the calling composition. The mask part is editable/keyframeable, transformable and can be combined; mask strength and reference-cell alpha govern coverage (sum >=255 visibility as documented). Boundary is not antialiased in this version. Nested instance source masks are documented as ignored; do not assume arbitrary recursive masks. Tutorial also documents effects visible only over a selected character shape and background wipe. No tutorial animation or original files inspected; this is primary text documentation, not source-level audit of the player. SpriteStudio 6's 2017 release adds masking and mesh/bones, confirming time-specific feature introduction.

Official Unity-Native conversion guide documents SS6Player 1.0.18/1.0.28-era limits: no instances in that mode, mask-target selection ignored, local scale/vertex deformation not applied to mask parts and strength near zero may differ. This is not a claim about all current player versions. Official SS6PlayerForUnity repository located but not read in this pass; no source-level mask/stencil or callback behavior claimed. Export guide returned 503 (indexed snippet only). SpriteStudio-specific masks are not panel semantics or automatically equivalent to arbitrary vector clipping. Production requires qualifying import mode and appearance, not merely successful serialization.

## C. Japanese commercial production: Gravity Daze 2

CEDEC 2017 scenario designer Naoko Sato as reported by GAME Watch and Famitsu distinguishes three presentation forms: comic demos (hand-drawn one-off art; drama and imagery with little game asset reuse), movie demos (3D action and reusable game assets), conversation demos (face windows, balloons and text; inexpensive revision). One-off comic cuts can need redrawing under gameplay/specification changes. The sequel's conversation-demo tool supported interactive preview, window/balloon transitions, writing and registering text plus SE/BGM; speaker reports over 1,000 demos produced and distributed writing responsibility. This is a concrete production tradeoff between bespoke composition and reusable authoring, not controlled productivity data. Not the same tool as the comic-demo panel editor; the source does not disclose the latter's serialized schema. Previous PN04 staff testimony addresses comic demos separately. The series' declared bande dessinée influence is not direct Japanese manga lineage.

## D. Consolidated requirements (candidate only)

1. Role-specific master source support, layer identity, transforms and registration, authored local panel/viewport, separate crop/clip/mask, safe anchors, screen reference coordinates and responsive crop variants.
2. Independent frame/layout, camera, scene artwork, mask/coverage, text and audio properties; explicit ownership of z-order and render isolation. Choose independent scene rendering only if useful; simple masks over reusable art may be cheaper.
3. Distinct progression target, rendered interpolated state, wall clock, simulation clock and represented events. Document hold, interruption, reversal, seek and duplicate/suppressed events.
4. Keep simultaneous visibility, narrative/reading relations and persistent illustration separate. Author/editor approves changed emphasis. Compare full composition, clipping, transition/reveal and final cue legibility; versioned source/runtime export checks.
5. Select presentation regime according to narrative purpose, asset reuse, revision burden and measured rendering budget, not a requirement that all narrative become motion comics or all effects become cameras.
