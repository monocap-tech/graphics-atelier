# G02-PANEL-01 — Manga panel morphology, sequence and visual grammar

2026-11-19. Parallel exploratory intake adjacent to G02-APERTURE-01–03 and G06 effects semantics. R1 bounded textual observations, R0 research agenda and proposed transfer. No panel corpus visually inspected, measurements acquired, observer tests or executable prototype. Not a new active series or replacement for the existing G02 cursor.

## Question

Study manga paneling on its own terms before abstracting it into game presentation. How do the distribution, geometry, border treatment, layering, composition, reading order, and relation to page/spread produce temporal, dramatic and attentional meaning? Aperture geometry enables but does not explain semantic use. Treat the page/spread as a composition and a sequence, not as an array of independent viewports.

## Initial source observations

* Shogakukan newcomer training (two parts): differentiates コマ割り (arrangement of size, shape, order) from 構図 (image composition inside a panel); recommends unambiguous right-to-left/top-to-bottom routing for typical right-bound pages, relative size contrast, spread-level review, and selected irregular panels. Suggested narrow 4–6 mm/wide 8–10 mm gutters are instructional conventions, not universal production standards. Part 2 distinguishes bleed, borderless treatment, full/partial spreads and diagonal/trapezoidal panels, addressing print gutter/trim and readable dialogue. Text read; figures not inspected.
* Natsume Fusanosuke's 2007 lecture and 2008 lecture 18 distinguish time segmentation, compression/release, page turns, and interaction of figure, lettering and backgrounds/effects. The lecture describes overlapping and enclosing panels, particularly the shōjo developments of the 1970s, as permitting simultaneity and recollection via weakened temporal segmentation. These are analyst interpretations, not experimental audience evidence.
* Holt/Fukuda's 2020 translation of Natsume's 1997 shōjo chapter (repository abstract only): the author treats nested/layered panels, reduction of compression/release, and symbolic 間白 as developments relative to earlier panel grammars, and argues these techniques crossed readership categories by the 1990s. Full chapter not read.
* Watsuki Nobuhiro, Jump SQ interview: tall characters tend to demand tall frames; wide panels restore page spaciousness and help text reading. Watsuki names Togashi Yoshihiro's influence. Primary composition is set at ネーム, including a priority panel and story hooks across the spread and turn. Individual practice, not industry-wide rule.
* Furudate Haruichi, Jump Manga Award interview 5: describes diagonal panels with parallel lines as smooth speed/rhythm, alternating disordered diagonals as disturbed play, and a specific contrast between preceding pressure/noise and an open quiet long shot across the page. A group of small panels within that example has no intended time succession and may be read in either direction. He and his editor discuss adapting spread composition to smartphone readers; Furudate identifies an advertising flyer as the source of his parallel diagonal pattern. This is firsthand design rationale, not measured attention or verified panel-image inspection.
* METI exhibition text on pre- and postwar manga: Norakuro and New Treasure Island often use three wide rows, with variations in size; 1934 Arai examples already used irregular yet readable arrangements. Avoid singular invention claims for Tezuka; verify individual editions and actual printed pages before historical dating of techniques.
* Manga no Genri (大場渉・森薫・入江亜季, KADOKAWA 2025): contents explicitly include time, フリウケ, stacking, 跳ね上げ, page turn, action, size contrast, irregular frames, gaze direction. Book metadata/TOC read only; detailed arguments remain R0.

## Independent dimensions of the future atlas (candidate)

| Axis | Examples / fields |
|---|---|
| Geometric form | rectangular regular, wide, tall, large/small relative to neighbors, skewed/trapezoid, irregular, inset shapes |
| Boundary and composition | framed, bleed 断ち切り, open/borderless, break-through ぶち抜き, contained or overlapping frames, masks, shared backgrounds, foreground figure across boundaries |
| Page organization | tier/grid, vertical/horizontal stacking, inset, full or partial spread, 4-koma/strip, page turn, vertical scrolling |
| Material delivery | manuscript vs publication geometry, binding gutter, trim, page vs spread vs phone cropping, reflow or fixed layout |
| Image grammar | long/medium/close framing, point of view, movement vector, character scale, text position, empty/abstract background, repeated motif |
| Sequence relation | moment progression, jump, simultaneous viewpoints, remembered event, interiority, stillness/ellipsis, scene transition, ambiguous or partial reading order |
| Intended effect | emphasis, quiet, speed/rhythm, tension/release, hesitation, reveal, reaction, disorientation, atmosphere, humor, symbolic space |

Axes are orthogonal enough for annotation but not independent in artistic practice; the categories are provisional and non-exclusive. Keep source's Japanese vocabulary and its historical context, avoid back-translating every technique into an assumed standard label. A large panel can present an instant; a small panel can depict years. No monotonic area→duration or angle→emotion law. Store maker testimony separately from analyst and observer hypotheses.

## Bounded future corpus

Sample specific lawful editions/spreads/strips with known reading orientation and physical/digital format; choose cases across prewar/early postwar, 1960s–70s shōnen and shōjo, seinen/gekiga, comedy/4-koma, action/sports, quiet everyday/psychological drama, doujin and modern smartphone reading/vertical scroll. These are overlapping sampling strata, not fixed genre rules. Start small: one regular sequence and one contrasting expressive sequence, then a shōjo layering example and a fixed four-panel form, with additional corpora only when they answer questions. Annotate page and spread separately, frame polygons/border visibility/bleed, area ratios and relative asymmetry, gutters per pair, intended reading precedences (partial order allowed), shared image support and layering, text/face/sound-lettering anchors, depicted vs narrated time, observation versus stated purpose. Preserve publication page context. Use lawful reference links and own diagrams/measurements; do not redistribute copyrighted art.

Candidate game correspondence: nested/irregular masks, time-staged panels or cut-ins, state-dependent foreground/background dominance, contrast in occupancy/visual load, reveal/hold scheduling, optional simultaneous viewpoints. No panel-duration inference from physical area, no general claim that the reader follows an observed eye path, no assertion manga semantics are identical to camera editing or UI. G06 comparison may use these concepts only when tied to the specific effect/scene question; G02's aperture succession remains source-frame-to-runtime mapping. Optional later original comparison: same authored content and event order under regular vs contrasted vs overlapping arrangements; distinguish readability and stated intent from observer response. No implementation commissioned.
