# G06-F09 — Game effects as executable art: arcade, indie/doujin and real-time production

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status: bounded source-led R1 investigation plus independent READ-ONLY inspection of four public third-party-mirrored game source files; no original output, engine/editor run, gameplay capture, viewer study or selected art target.** This follows [F01–F04 manga](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-04.md), [F05 effects animation](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-05.md), [F06 articulated illustration](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-06.md), [F07 genealogical evidence](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-07.md) and [F08 natural phenomena](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-08.md). [STD01](../docs/EFFECTS_STANDARD.md) remains a later production assessment, not an automatic command to build another toy.

## Question

What makes a *game effect* a game-production artifact rather than simply a moving graphic? **Answer sought on sources' own terms:** where the artwork and authored timing live; which game events and cameras govern them; what visual properties must coexist with play; which hardware/asset formats/runtime limitations force choices; who can edit, revise and validate them; and how independent developers build alternatives. Treat *image/representation, event/condition, scene, presentation order, output machine and labor* as interconnected without equating them. Distinguish an **effect asset** (texture/flipbook/geometry/graph), an **effect instance** (timed live object), **gameplay state** (hit, collision, warning, scored event), **post-process/screen treatment**, and an **artist's intended impression**. They can overlap; the game itself defines their authority.

**Crucial contrast:** for much modern production a particle tool is the standard authoring interface, but Japanese homebrew/doujin games have also generated effects through direct code and 2D/3D render operations without any dedicated VFX editor. Neither is a fixed national or historical progression.

## Three bounded cases worth further investigation

### F09-V01 — Arcade *Space Harrier*: the effect is part of a perspective/gameplay contract

Suzuki (F09-S01) explains that graphics memory and scaling ruled out the original military aircraft and intended smoke/fire/explosion sophistication; the appearance of the whole world was redesigned as a **production-compatible artistic solution**. Character size and animation frame count, fantasy mushroom forms, checkerboard ground, dynamic backgrounds, motion seat, gradients and sound contribute to expressive perception, although no audience experiment is established.

Independent IGCC indexed prose (F09-S03) describes a **multiphase shot consequence**: contact→falling/ongoing explosion→ground-contact explosion pattern, and separate fair-death information; the independent gameplay critic's observation needs confirmation via **exact 1985-arcade gameplay capture and documented event/sprite timing**, not a claimed original Sega state machine. IGCC fully fetched prose F09-S04 reports lock-on ping and later homing, making *play-state feedback, aural mark, projectile visual and target interaction* a candidate **single directed episode** for observation.

**A reviewable target would be a 5–10-s continuous encounter** containing aim/ping/shot/contact/fall/ground-residue and player avoidance, then segmentation by different world depth and camera scale; do not pick exact frame counts, palette values or sprite origins from screenshots. Distinguish *what a critic observed* from a verified original 1985 sprite implementation. This is **reference study only**, not a mandate to recreate the game or authorize any asset extraction.

### F09-V02 — Noiz2sa: coded construction, blending and foreground authority

**Exactly opened third-party mirrored C files, version-qualified** in F09-S06:
- `src/frag.c` (pool, randomized shape and velocity, lifespan, per-event spawners) shows the visual aftermath controlled by individual integer/fixed-ish state and per-frame direct drawing.
- `src/noiz2sa.c` state-dependent `draw()` composes background, foes, bullet wakes and fragments, blends, then explicitly draws player and hostile bullets on top. **The same procedural effect is not given unlimited right to cover bullet cores**.
- `src/screen.c` `blendScreen()` uses lookup `colorAlp[l1buf[i]][l2buf[i]]`; `smokeScreen()` uses warped previous buffer and attenuation. This is a concrete **raster/palette feedback** method, neither hand-drawn each-frame VFX nor a shader/editor particle graph.
- `src/shot.c` maps coordinates to symmetric moving `drawBox` pairs modulated by a sine table, so visual projectile is not necessarily a stored shot sprite.

**Read-only source inspection, not output/fidelity check.** Third-party repo's 2016 screen-file modification and initial 2002 Cho copyrights mean it is evidence of an inspectable historical-derived codebase, not audit of unchanged original binaries; check original source archives, build config/licensing and compare output if advancing to R2/R3. Gameplay collision and bullet core rendering remain separately reviewable from frags. Do not deduce a universal optimal foreground drawing order; this code's choice is one independent STG implementation.

### F09-V03 — Contemporary authored scenes are edited **against the rest of the game**

Professional reports supply concrete variants of one challenge: *maintain artistic impact while gameplay and actors stay readable.*
- **CC2 2023** uses composition of main/support/decorative effects, differentiated departure times and actual background with simultaneous FX (S10).
- **Last Cloudia 2019** preserves *pixel-art actor visibility* while combining cutscene camera direction, SPARK GEAR palette effects, Spine illustration and existing asset libraries (S12).
- **SINoALICE 2017** uses economical single-channel textures and palette mapping for weapon/element/power variants amid simultaneous effects (S13).
- **Berserk or Die 2025 indie** chooses near-white large shapes and six-frame peak as **personal style/iteration strategy**, not best-practice universal timing (S14).
- **Danmakufu v2→ph3** makes **render priority and effect objects** public, enabling author scripts to place forms relative to player and bullets, with version caveats (S07).
- **SEGA 2019** and **CC2 2023** incorporate source observation, breakdown, effect materials and actual stage context, not merely software presets (S09/S10).

**Negative comparison:** particle editors are a prevalent studio method, but Noiz2sa and Danmakufu show game art can be generated as hand-written draws and image-processing operations instead. Simulation/baking from F08 *House of Dead Scarlet Dawn* shows yet another source→runtime conversion. No claim that one is historically a successor to the other or that matching an arbitrary screenshot requires an engine's particle component.

## Practical distinctions retained from F01–F09

| Production concern | Manga/anime counterpart | Distinct game-level condition |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | Page, panel, sequence/cut | Camera and viewport change during input; actors/projectiles and HUD appear simultaneously |
| Timing | Manga panel disclosure; anime exposure sheet | Trigger, branch, interrupt, cancel, persistence, update step vs display frame |
| Art/media | Ink/tone; original drawings/photography; illustration layers | Sprite/atlas, artist-made animated texture, code geometry, meshes, particles, baked simulation, shaders |
| Visibility | Manga gaze/balloon priority; film shot staging | Collision-critical bullets/telegraphs and in-game actions must remain readable while decorative effects overlap |
| Authority | Storyboard and authored final cut | Gameplay state/collision typically authoritative **where needed**; some FX are ambient/autonomous, or may intentionally misdirect |
| Editor and custody | Source art/timesheet/project → final render | Creator/DCC/editable graph + runtime import, exact color/blend/scaling/depth + revision and performance budgets |
| Repeatability | Identical reel/page | Effect may be instantiated, interrupted, layered or drawn from random states; compare reproducible seed/representative state |

**Important nonclaim:** graphic brightness or shape is **not** generally equivalent to a hitbox; a shooter with separate fixed collision cores may intentionally draw a larger decorative glow, while a fighting game can demand stricter tracking. Conversely the *game* cannot be treated as one immutable cinematic composition; establish a bounded representative view before evaluation.

## Open evidence and stopping rule

We have **more real production evidence** than before (including direct public C source and named modern artist-tool flows) but still lack:
- Primary 1985 Sega graphics/pattern masters, effect timing tables, source code or independent full arcade captures; *Space Harrier* indexed critic's ground-explosion interpretation awaits sequence confirmation.
- An original licensed/deliberately authored FX specimen in a real tool with editable assets, contextual playback and revision; existing technical miniatures do not close FX-A/S/C/R/I/X.
- Confirmed historical path manga→anime→AM2 effects workforce/asset pipeline; Suzuki attests visual influence and hardware limits, not studio method transfer.
- Actual original game effect asset and audio variation from a precisely identified source (arcade ROM vs console/SEGA AGES); distinguish screenshots from period originals.
- Tested doujin rendering source/efficiency in the intended display contract, or actual audience experience beyond creator opinion.

**Next bounded research decision (F10 candidate, not automatically executed):** perform **first original-arcade source/sequence audit** for one *Space Harrier* firing/hit/fall/ground aftermath episode (ideally accompanied by lock-on and sound); register original and later ports separately, capture at native/display scale and pause/reference frame intervals, distinguish visible observation from inferred engine state. Compare that with **one** independent source-code example (Noiz2sa) and **one** art-editor workflow (Last Cloudia/SEGA/CC2) specifically to identify transferable requirements, not identical art. Only then decide on an editable production target; do not launch a new semantics microfixture or invent a general-purpose effects DSL. G07 deferred, VIGIL held and P07/RT01 debt conditional on reuse.
