# G06-F08 — Material phenomena: observation, drawn causality, stylization and simulation

Date: 2026-11-18. **Status: bounded R1 source-led study, with publicly displayed reference drawings and production stills identified but NO independently timed finished sequences.** Continues [F01–F04 manga](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-04.md), [F05 drawn effects](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-05.md), [F06 illustration articulation](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-06.md), [F07 historical crossings](G06-FUNDAMENTALS-07.md). This pass examines “realistic” effects as **one family of craft challenges** and does not assume realistic image = physically correct calculation or visually better effect. No simulation, hand drawing, live engine test, audience research, paid book purchase or commissioned assets. [STD01](../docs/EFFECTS_STANDARD.md) remains eventual production acceptance, not an immediate order to manufacture another scene.

## Question / working distinction

How do effects animators learn physical causation and appearance, then deliberately choose contours, scale, phase, timing, exposure, residue and scene relation to make it **readable, expressive and consistent**? The source set shows that (1) natural reference, (2) physically informed staged exaggeration, (3) visual convention/style, (4) authoring technique and (5) physical computation have overlapping but non-identical roles.

**Do not make another universal effects taxonomy.** Describe each **source episode** in terms of what phenomenon is studied, what feature is preserved or revised, how it is produced, and why the author says the change is useful; distinguish descriptive expert judgment from empirical audience evidence. The same work can combine drawing, compositing, simulation and gameplay state. Avoid assigning concrete fluid-physics laws, temperatures or fluid-solver settings without independently grounded references.

## Four bounded source events

### F08-V01 — Underwater blast: *physical causal chain plus invented flash*

**Source:** Ozawa, F08-S01. **Physical relationships stated:** water propelled upward; pulls apart and falls; spray/shore-facing ripple field persists; settled water is final residue. **Authorial departures:** initial T光 flash for emphasis, foreground waves accentuated, vapor/spray assembled as separate compositing material when desired. **Claim supported:** trained animator differentiates phenomena he believes physically motivated from deliberately invented cinematic legibility. **Not supported:** a pressure-wave fluid model, actual physics calculation, film audience response, or precise time intervals. **Lesson:** a visually credible event can include a knowingly nonphysical component so long as its causal/dramatic construction is legible.

### F08-V02 — Yasuhiko → Masuda: **the missing original timesheet**

**Source:** F08-S06, original interview; film *Gundam: Cucuruz Doan’s Island* (2022), older effects style reference *Crusher Joe* (1983) and Yasuhiko’s *Gundam* work. Masuda reports **visible reference** (repeated films/art books), **intent** (soft/rounded/some red-pink explosions rather than today's sharper practice), **attempt** (redraw for 2022), **blocker** (images contain no exposure timing), then late **direct custody from source practitioner** (Yasuhiko rough original drawings **and timesheets**) revealing inbetweens at earliest expansion. Yasuhiko's partial re-entry into the production is a much stronger mechanism than form resemblance alone; still, sheets themselves are not deposited here. The fact that the final style could be partially recovered while critical timing stayed hidden demonstrates why collecting still frames and an operations vocabulary cannot substitute for interviewing or observing actual artist practice.

Masuda's further opinion that Yasuhiko's work had once been *realistic* for its time, then became codified and developed in later effects lineages is **historical creator interpretation**; we have not independently audited a broad film corpus. The temporally revealed technical decision is specific and useful even if the bigger history is incomplete.

### F08-V03 — Same film, two competing 'realism' methods

**Source:** F08-S07, *Gundam SEED FREEDOM*. **One production:** explosions were designed to resemble **artistic drawing**, not unconstrained simulation; separate compositing AOVs, additional hand-drawn detail and manually shaped light/fades supported per-cut art direction. **Another:** water splash used an actual FLIP fluid cache; when a needed backward spray did not occur in that simulation, an artificial driver object was inserted. **Story difference:** stronger water splash marks the less skillful/rougher landing; lesser splash for smoother, more experienced ship. The same production therefore has **drawn-image realism** and **physically informed fluid realism**, both requiring staged deviations. No external viewers tested and no exact solver config replicated. The distinction is between **targeted visual causality** and physically faithful self-sufficiency, not between 'good art' and 'bad simulation'.

### F08-V04 — Material simulation as an editable ingredient, not a final autonomous answer

**Sources:** F08-S08 (*House of the Dead: Scarlet Dawn*, 2018, arcade), F08-S10 (Colopl, 2025), F08-S11 (Shirogumi, 2026). All explicitly separate **calculation** from **staging / timing / character path / compositing / runtime export**. Houdini can author a water cache, explosion library, screen-relative camera material, mesh animation or texture sequence; a game engine can render baked artifacts rather than solve underlying equations live. Colopl found hand-keyed timing necessary for a directed collapse; Shirogumi planned a quasi-character gas path and even reversed a cache to achieve authored reconvergence. These are **independent, dated cases** with different goals, not the claim that their budgets/tools transfer to our project. Art-direction control remains essential even when numerical simulation is physically sophisticated.

## What the term “realistic” must not obscure

- **Physical correspondence:** Does something follow known material/causal behavior? A simulated result might be internally consistent and still omit a visually necessary attribute due to finite resolution, boundary or unmodeled effects.
- **Perceptual/representational plausibility:** Does the image make viewers recognize a phenomenon, material, scale or momentum? Expert author judgment can target it, but reception is unmeasured unless specifically tested.
- **Style / historical normalcy:** In one decade a round, colorful explosion may count as 'realistic' relative to prevailing shorthand; in another it may be called retro/stylized. Masuda's historical judgment is a hypothesis about conventions and training, not a quantified time series.
- **Scene efficacy:** Does the event make its cause, consequence, dramatic value and relevant character readable without losing the desired visual force? A highly detailed fluid may harm composition; an intentionally 'unreal' flash may clarify the event.
- **Production method:** Did an artist draw it, composite layers, render procedural texture, simulate offline, or run a particle field live? The method does **not** determine any of the preceding values from the finished image alone.

This is a comparison checklist, **not** a new research maturity ladder or complete realism taxonomy.

Preserve contrasts between AM2-style sprite hardware contracts and modern engines; use *House of the Dead: Scarlet Dawn* as a real arcade-context later comparison, **not** a proxy for *Space Harrier*’s original implementation. Establish at least one exact legally viewable source sequence and editor/artist workflow before calling a method ready for an original production target. Do not silently choose shaders, Effekseer, Live2D or Unity just because they appear in source material.
