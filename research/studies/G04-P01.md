# G04-P01 — Mechanical corridor, reusable environment materials

Proposed, not executed. Goal: achieve structural richness and directional motion behind gameplay using geometry economy, not reproduce a commercial stage. Targets: Ikaruga industrial scenery and Astebreed mechanical stage direction; exact lawful footage, version and time interval pending. No fidelity claim until registered.

## Local fixture and workflow

- Design an original 6-piece kit: wall panel, rib, conduit, vent, rotating assembly, landmark gate. Cross-section 12x8 arbitrary units; 4 bays of 6 units longitudinally; test a 12 s camera path with straight approach and oblique reveal. Camera and bullet proxies precede detailing. Declare which surfaces are actually visible, and intentional quiet area behind projectiles.
- Blender: block out, apply scale/consistent origins, mark hard edges and face orientation, record triangulated counts. Initial fixture budget 2,400 unique kit triangles, 12,000 visible instanced triangles, one 256x256 RGB color atlas plus optional explicit effects texture; not Dreamcast/Saturn limits. Profile actual renderer separately. No paid tool dependencies.
- Build original atlas first at 256x256 (Aseprite or Blender image editor): swatches/gradients, repeating panel trim, narrow edge strip, vent tile, unique warning/landmark. Keep repeated strip along wrap axis; verify seam and sampling compatibility. Atlas internal tiles require explicit wrapping or geometry segmentation; do not assume whole-image wrap repeats an arbitrary subregion. Gutter or inset according to filter/mip tests; document chosen texel density per unit.
- A: shared swatches/gradients only. B: same geometry and camera, add authored panel seams, vent details, directional highlights using trim atlas; simple unlit/baked-style colors first. C (optional): additional geometry or material passes as control, justified by visible benefit. Do not alter multiple things without recording them.
- Repeat kit in original scene and export FBX or glTF as supported by selected host; document axis/unit transforms, textures, sampling, material, culling and render order. Use Unity to test look, not as proof of hardware behavior. Compare 320x180 and 640x360 local logical composites, integer upscale, nearest vs filtered/mips on textures separately from final upscale. Match camera and gameplay proxy layers.
- Compare at fixed frames and in motion: silhouette/readability, landmark recognition, apparent seams, repetition, temporal aliasing, contrast with bullets, revision time, unique texture bytes and triangulated counts. Verify atlas update affects all intended instances; no arbitrary numerical quality score. Budget 3–4 operator hours for initial A/B; record blocker if unmet, not a completion estimate.

Memory accounting example: a 256x256 RGBA8 uncompressed base level consumes 262,144 bytes, while six unique same-size maps consume 1,572,864 bytes; 1 shared map instead of six saves 1,310,720 bytes in that restricted comparison. RGB24 is 196,608 bytes per map. Actual GPU storage depends on format/import/mips/compression; atlas density/workload differs and batching is not guaranteed.

Gate: original editable kit, documented UV/texture mappings, reproducible export, no unintended visible tile seams under chosen camera/filter, acceptable background/gameplay separation, actual measurements and A/B revisions. Record failures. Transfer kit logic to a different silhouette/location before R4.
