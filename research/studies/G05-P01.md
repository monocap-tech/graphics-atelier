# G05-P01 — Screen-relative gameplay and camera-directed 3D

Status: isolated projection control executed; engine study proposed. No game renderer, scene or external code exercised. Reference: SRC011 and SRC077.

## Projection control

Pinhole camera orbits origin at radius 10 and looks at it (yaw 0–90 deg, no roll, pitch zero). Local viewport 640x360, vertical FOV 60 deg. Five fixed screen targets (center, quartet at x=160/480 and y=90/270). Compare (1) world anchors fixed at z=0 from baseline, (2) points recomputed on camera-relative forward-depth 10 plane, (3) screen rays intersecting fixed world z=0 plane. Rays are not normalized; scalar is forward depth. Positive intersections only; epsilon 1e-9. Camera at 90 deg lies in the plane; zero-depth and parallel hits rejected. Roundtrip values rounded to 9 decimals, pass threshold 1e-8 px; ideal algebra, not measured runtime precision.

Run `node labs/G05-P01/projection_control.mjs`. The same pure function was run in the JS sandbox (not Node CLI); five checks passed.

This exposes the difference between screen invariance, a fixed-world plane constraint and camera-relative depth. At 60 deg the fixed plane requires depth 90 for a sample; at 75 deg two rays fail; at 90 deg all invalid under defined rule. Stable projected center does not preserve world velocity, object size, orientation, physical contacts, occlusion, continuity or gameplay by itself. Camera-relative projection is a mathematical candidate, not a reconstruction of Sanglard or Treasure source.
