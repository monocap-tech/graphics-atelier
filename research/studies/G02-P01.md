# G02-P01 — Illustration adaptation and proportional asset architecture

Status: proposed, 2026-11-18. R0–R1 source observations only. No specimens produced, commissioned, downloaded or tested. Domains VG01, VG02, VG06, VG07, VG08, IP01; bridges G01/G03 and G09.

## Linked feature-survival evidence

[G02-FEATURE-01](../G02-FEATURE-01.md) records a new bounded corpus of Japanese artist technique, same-character simplification, illustration layer/compositing comparisons, computational pixel abstraction and a low-resolution diagnostic; it does not establish a preferred native size, observer response, or original *Space Harrier* distance-based LOD rule. Preserve the original-art test and explicit design/rights handoff below as the governing experiment, and leave the Suzuki/AM2 reverse-engineering thread separate.

## Question

How can one procure or author an illustrated character, determine the minimum viable raster representation at a specified camera, articulate it into a reusable pose library, and deliver assets compatible with the display and surrounding world? Treat procurement and conversion as upstream parts of graphics architecture, not external prerequisites of unknown specification.

## Comparative reference library

Target properties rather than style imitation: Inti Creates (Mega Man Zero/ZX, Gunvolt/iX; compact illustration fidelity); classic SNK/Capcom arcade (anatomy, timing, deformation); KOF XII/XIII (model-assisted reconstruction); original Melty Blood vs Under Night and Type Lumina (low/HD hand-drawn representation); Guilty Gear X/XX and BlazBlue (high-density and HD production). Add Japanese independent MUGEN/doujin practitioners, adjacent animation/print conventions and non-Japanese examples including Skullgirls. These are provisional categories, not mutually exclusive or proven general studio lineages. See SRC001–003 and SRC032–043 in [registry](../SOURCES.md).

Record one entry per specific title/version/platform/camera/pose, recording:
- Evidence location, source type, date, language, inspection/access/rights status, confidence and measurement reproducibility.
- Logical viewport and actual gameplay viewport (exclude letterbox and non-world HUD where appropriate); final output resolution, zoom and scaling; camera angle, posture and baseline.
- Native asset visible bounds if lawfully known, source or frame dimensions if documented, displayed bounds from an unmodified capture, palette/edge method, head height and landmark annotations. Never infer native asset size from an upscaled capture without proving the scale.
- Screen occupancy, head/body proportions, relative character heights, stage objects, motion reach, frame count and unique drawing count when known. Keep uncertainty, occlusion, weapon/hair bounds and scaling artifacts explicit.
- Art-to-runtime workflow and evidence class. A public screenshot may support measurement, not a claim about a studio's layered masters or internal authoring settings.

Do not redistribute commercial assets or require extraction; use lawful captures/reference links, own measurements and original examples. Initial R1 observations: SRC035 uses an approximate metric-like grid relative to KOF compatibility; SRC036 describes screen-relative size and stylization together. Neither implies a shared industry standard.

## Proportional model (candidate)

Define logical gameplay viewport height V (px), reference character world height H (m or abstract units), native upright visible height h (px), head height q (px), and display density d (logical screen px/native sprite px at the baseline camera). Set s=h/H, n=h/q, rho=d*h/V, k=source drawing height/h. These are independent decisions only until dependencies are fixed: h=rho*V/d and s=h/H. If perspective/nonuniform scale applies, record depth, transform and separate axes; do not apply a single scalar across the scene. An output upscale multiplies screen and displayed height together; it does not add native detail.

Use consistent registration (ground/contact plane, world origin, attachment anchors); distinguish anatomical height from hair, weapon and attack extents. Record a shared world scale plus explicitly documented stylization exceptions, rather than impose identical head sizes or literal meters on all designs.

Investigate candidate occupancy fractions (1/8, 1/6, 1/5, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 3/5, 1/phi) against empirical distributions and gameplay controls. Investigate golden-section composition, 1:sqrt(2) A/JIS B paper modularity, animation layout frame vs paper, and metric reference grids as separate hypotheses. Compare against genre, hardware, camera, aspect ratio, integer grids and simple fractions; control for selection bias and measurement uncertainty. No evidence presently establishes a golden-ratio rule or causal paper-to-game lineage. Do not substitute geometrical numerology for measured legibility.

## Procurement/commission and layer handoff (candidate brief)

One design may serve gameplay sprite, portrait, cut-in illustration, 3D reference/model or other approved representations. Specify which are required and which are optional before authoring or requesting quotes. Use internally authored or suitably licensed designs first.

| Input / deliverable | Acceptance and custody |
|---|---|
| Character brief | narrative identity, silhouette, relative heights, explicit details that must survive conversion, allowable exaggeration/omission, asymmetry/handedness |
| Model/reference sheets (設定画・三面図・身長対比表) | neutral front/profile/rear and three-quarter if needed, anatomy landmarks, costume construction/occluded details, expression and equipment callouts, shared baseline |
| High-resolution editable master | layered body construction, costume, equipment, contours, colors, shading, registration and notes; separate source resolution and output framing; agree on tool/native formats and neutral interchange exports |
| Articulation reference | chosen key poses and facing directions, anchors and movement constraints; distinguish design reference from executable animation |
| Low-resolution editable counterpart | reduced guide (not authoritative final art), silhouette, clusters/lines, facial adjustments, material ramps, independent parts/effects where justified, registration; source and palette versions tracked |
| Export package | lossless raster frames, native bounds, origin, anchors, durations, facing/mirror policy, optional hit/hurtbox references separate from visible art, atlas/import metadata and renderer settings |
| Rights and revision handoff | provenance, creator, attribution, license or negotiated adaptation/commercial/derivative/use scope, editable source delivery, permitted sharing, acceptance and revision rounds; no implied transfer of copyright, permission to modify or right to redistribute |

No artist is contacted or contract entered. This brief is a research template, not legal advice or a fixed universal deliverables list. Validate handoff with a self-authored asset before actual external procurement, and record revisions/time. Keep commercial source artwork out of public repository absent permission.

## Bounded original specimen

Use one original character of moderate costume complexity, with an identifying face/hair detail, asymmetrical equipment and articulated limbs; a second distinct design tests transfer. Select a single neutral and three motion-stress poses initially (stride, extended attack, foreshortened/recoil), not an entire animation set. Record identity markers and acceptable loss before testing. Select target camera/gameplay constraints before drawing; separate two comparisons:
1. At equal displayed height/occupancy, construct and manually clean native heights (e.g. 48/72/96/128 px) from the same source; match final on-screen size and document resampling/zoom to isolate native information density.
2. At fixed native density, vary viewport/camera occupancy to evaluate screen composition, world scale, environmental fit, motion envelope and visual competition with effects/HUD.

Candidate baseline, not a required standard: 640×360 logical world viewport, 1.75 m reference height, 48 native px/m, 84 px upright height, d=1, seven-head anatomy (12 px/head), occupancy ~23.3%; 128×128 registered working cell and 4×/8× source cell (512/1024 px square). Compare other regimes including 4:3 where the target warrants it. Test direct raster drawing against adapted illustration; use G03 model assistance only where it answers a specific question. Distinguish optical/color correction from raw reduction.

After choosing a promising regime, build a registered starter library: neutral turnarounds; locomotion contact/passing/extension; launch/apex/fall/land; crouch/dash; combat anticipation/impact/recovery; reaction and equipment attachment. Scope an initial subset (e.g. 8–12 keys) subject to available skill and time; full 20–30 pose coverage is a candidate later extension, not an initial requirement. Include a short ordered motion test. Do not mirror asymmetrical elements by default.

Frame cell and sheet dimensions derive from maximum required envelopes relative to origin (including negative offsets), not from standing height alone. With fixed cell W×H, C columns, R rows and intercell gutter g, unpadded sheet bounds are CW+(C-1)g by RH+(R-1)g. Variable bounds/trimmed atlas are acceptable if pivot offsets and frame timing survive. Record source-to-frame coordinate mapping, transparent margins, cropping, packing padding and native/output sampling independently. A large attack/effect may live in an auxiliary layer with its own bounds. Choose exact asset/export schemas in response to the tested runtime.

## Acceptance and transfer

Before drawing, declare target-specific thresholds for readable cues in neutral and stress poses at final viewing scale, anatomy/anchor consistency, preservation or intentional abstraction of character identity, motion legibility, environmental fit and manual labor budget. Record per-pose hours, revisions, unique drawing count, memory/atlas cost and rejected scales. Compare under matched backgrounds and motion; a single attractive idle drawing is insufficient. Do not assert viewer preferences without appropriate evaluation.

Gate 1: small comparative records from selected low-footprint, classic arcade, model-assisted and HD references (at least one usable instance per class or documented blocker), plus an independent workflow source.

Gate 2: original layered source, native resolution/contact-sheet comparisons, measurements and explicit selected/rejected regime with reasons. Gate 3: registered pose subset, short animation and clean export/import in a test host; repeat one approved design or attachment change and verify propagation. Gate 4: reproduce the contract with the second distinct character or representation, recording deviations and costs. G09 integration consumes only qualified outputs, not exploratory defaults.

Stop or revise if measurements cannot distinguish asset from display scale, rights cannot support the intended deliverables, key visual properties cannot survive the selected scale, or effort is disproportionate. A bounded failure can determine a better next experiment. No proof of a Japanese universal system or a Western deficiency is required; compare actual published methods and documented gaps.
