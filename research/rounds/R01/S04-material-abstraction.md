# GA-R01-S04 — Material and lighting cues under abstraction

**Status:** chartered / R0 (2026-11-18). The Japanese coloring-book observations in G02-FEATURE-01 are supporting source material, not results from a new sprite experiment.

**Core question:** What makes skin, fabric, hair, metal, glass, fur and gemstones remain perceptually distinguishable when spatial, palette, lighting, or motion budgets are severely reduced?

## Research territory

- Material-signaling cues: highlight geometry, contrast, hue relation, edge softness, transparency, reflection placement, local texture, temporal sparkle and implied thickness.
- Relation between source illustration method (アニメ塗り, 厚塗り, 水彩塗り, ギャルゲ塗り, 宝石塗り), raster cluster construction, display light, backgrounds and gameplay effects.
- Alternative materials with similar average colors, and one material under different illumination; avoid simply labeling a palette 'metal' or 'glass'.
- Explicitly distinguish physically simulated appearance, convention-based drawn appearance, art-director intention, and what actual observers report.

## Opening questions

1. What is the minimum authored cue set for each material *in context*?
2. Which cues are stable under reduction; which can be substituted or temporally revealed?
3. When do highlights convey wetness, translucency, curvature, hardness or merely focal importance?
4. How do palettes, outline policies and final display filters modify those inferences?

## Evidence and methods

Begin with close Japanese page-level review of the privately supplied *キャラ塗り上達術 決定版* examples referenced in [G02-FEATURE-01](../../G02-FEATURE-01.md), without publishing page scans. Compare additional professional pixel-artist practice and visual perception/graphics literature. Make **original** paired swatches and a small in-scene object, varying one cue at a time with unchanged shape/lighting where feasible; evaluate at native and projected size. Keep effect-specific lighting/operator research in G06 separately.

## Outputs and limits

A material-cue matrix, original controlled raster plates and an account of failure cases are candidate outputs. Avoid a grand rule that a single reflection pattern means the same thing in all art styles. Do not infer the print-book's layer procedure was historically used for games.

**First bounded inquiry:** choose two visually confusable materials at one target size and document which controlled alterations make them more distinguishable, with author judgment separate from any viewer study.
