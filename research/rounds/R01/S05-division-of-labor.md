# GA-R01-S05 — Historical organization and artistic knowledge transfer

**Status:** chartered / R0 (2026-11-18); historical lead collection not yet a verified institutional genealogy.

**Core question:** How were illustration, game sprite art, background art, animation, compositing and art direction divided, supervised and taught across changing Japanese game-production organizations?

## Research territory

- Chronologies of game art jobs and interfaces: in-house departments, external illustrators, freelancers, publishers, animation studios, dot artists and promotional artists.
- Sprite-led vs illustration-led commissioning; shared style guide and art-director revision authority; informal mentorship vs formal tools and documentation.
- Concrete careers, studios, named titles and dated artifacts rather than conjecture about generalized 'Japanese tradition'.
- Knowledge-transfer failure, outsourcing, remake workflows, organizational costs, legal custody and missed continuity as research questions—not prior conclusions.

## Opening questions

1. When did game graphic artist, character designer and promotional illustrator become distinct roles in particular studios?
2. Who had the authority to change a character design for hardware or game-screen constraints?
3. What did 'shared sprite-art knowledge' actually consist of (documents, review cycles, tools, worked examples, apprenticeships)?
4. Which cross-company and cross-media claims can be traced through named people/projects instead of retrospective similarity?

## Evidence and methods

Source candidates raised in discussion include former SNK staff testimony, early Namco/Tower of Druaga accounts, Capcom designer recollections, KOF XII's official gallery and later HD remakes. Reopen exact primary or contemporary passages before making a dated claim. Cross-check interviews against credits, contemporaneous documentation, job adverts and production artifacts. Contrast testimony with surviving outputs, separating individual recollection from a studio-wide standard. The parallel Suzuki/AM2 investigation owns the detailed Sega technical reconstruction; this series may cite verified findings but does not duplicate it.

## Outputs and limits

A dated case-led organizational atlas, role/provenance graph, documented gaps and competing production routes are potential outputs. A broad national history, an unsupported claim of Western deficiency, or a definitive institutional lineage from a few interviews is out of scope.

**First bounded inquiry:** compare two specifically dated studio/title production cases, identify creators/role boundaries and at least one documented design handoff or approval, with source confidence stated.
