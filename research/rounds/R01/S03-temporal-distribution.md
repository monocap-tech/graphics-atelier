# GA-R01-S03 — Temporal distribution of visual detail

**Status:** chartered / R0 (2026-11-18); distinct from G03's general tooling and G06's effects investigations.

**Core question:** Which character properties are better disclosed over motion, pose changes, camera movement or staged revelation than encoded in an idle frame?

## Research territory

- Identity cues that exist only in turns, anticipation, contact, recoil, expression changes, prop use or transformation.
- Temporal salience: how many drawings and how long a cue must be visible; how exposure, looping, repetition, anticipation and accent frames influence legibility.
- Budgeting detailed illustrations vs compact sprites vs expressive animation; asynchronous view changes and asymmetric parts.
- Distinguish temporal detail *allocation*, animation grammar, visual effects, player feedback and collision logic; they overlap but are not one field.

## Opening questions

1. What can a short pose sequence communicate that a maximally detailed still cannot?
2. Which details become readable only at specific poses or dramatic instants?
3. How do production teams trade permanent costume complexity against short-lived animation detail?
4. How does moving viewing distance or camera competition change these choices?

## Evidence and methods

Verify the previously mentioned *World Flipper* developer remarks and relevant game/animation production documents at their actual passage and version; acquire lawful timed clips (not promo stills misidentified as animation). Compare the same authored character's still, turning/acting clip and one simplified alternative under identical playback. Record frame timing, output size, visual context and source-art or rig changes. Use [G02-P01](../../studies/G02-P01.md) for identity custody and G03 for registered key-pose methods; do not redirect the series into the separate Suzuki/AM2 inquiry.

## Outputs and limits

Candidate: a temporal feature ledger recording *when* identity, material, and narrative cues become legible, plus a minimal original animated comparison. A sequence with technical frame checks alone does not establish the intended experience. No need to build a complete animation system.

**First bounded inquiry:** one accessible production case where a character's design intentionally changes between idle and active poses, and a reproducible six-to-twelve-frame original contrast.
