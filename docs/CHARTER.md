# Research charter (provisional v0.2)

Original charter: 2026-11-17. Programme-level revision proposed: 2026-11-20. Existing investigations retain their historical criteria and evidence.

## Objective

Investigate, qualify and operationalize the relations between **artistic intention, representation, perception, computation, production and presentation** that make graphical quality possible in specified contexts. Recover independently usable capacity to originate, construct, judge, revise and sustain original graphical work with obtainable evidence and feasible tools. Selected Japanese commercial and doujin work remains a major case corpus, not the standard by which every technology, medium or production must be judged.

The object of reconstruction is production capability and its conditions, not a commercial asset, an entire studio history or a compulsory historical implementation. See [programme map](PROGRAMME.md) and [methods and quality](METHODS_AND_QUALITY.md).

## Quality and jurisdiction

A minimum acceptable graphical **outcome** is defined by its declared artistic and operational requirements, under representative viewing/use conditions. A minimum **productive capability** concerns an author's ability to make, inspect, correct and maintain such outcomes. Neither is a fixed resolution, stylistic canon, technology, amount of detail or cross-project numeric score. Requirements may differ by medium and intended experience; exceptions and intentional excess can be legitimate.

Mathematics and computation can establish formal properties and costs; perceptual studies can measure observer outcomes under test conditions; artistic practice can construct and critically revise intended qualities; production evidence can establish handoff/rework feasibility; historical sources can document situated methods and transmission. These jurisdictions interact but are not interchangeable. No lane's success proves another lane, and no single institution or discipline governs them all.

## Scope

Illustration specification, lawful asset sourcing/commissioning, adaptation and custody; comparative character/world/display proportions; raster construction; animation/articulation; polygonal environments; spatial composition; effects; illustrative/dramatic composition; rendering architecture; production infrastructure; and interface/presentation design. Other regional practices are legitimate comparators. The historical lineages must not be collapsed into one purported national style.

## Articulative constraints

| Constraint | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Accessibility | Publicly obtainable (including lawfully purchasable) evidence or independently reproducible experiment. Private institutional material is not a dependency. |
| Provenance | Identify origin, language, dates, version, source type, access and verification status. Keep claims separate from interpretation. |
| Historical distinction | A successful reconstruction does not establish how a studio originally produced an effect. |
| Operational completeness | Name tools, formats, parameters, intermediate artifacts, operations, and expected results. |
| Reproducibility | Preserve original specimens, settings, rerun instructions, and limitations. |
| Transfer | Demonstrate application to a distinct asset or context when the workflow is production-critical. |
| Comparative validation | Define target properties and controls before judging resemblance. |
| Feasibility | Record manual labor, runtime cost, skill, software and hardware requirements. |
| Asset continuity | Specify source art, usage and adaptation rights, reference dimensions, representation-specific deviations, approved pose/variant libraries and editable handoff. Preserve provenance from acquisition through runtime export. |
| Architectural coherence | Define interfaces between authoring and runtime without prematurely prescribing a single engine. |
| Independence | No ripped assets, inaccessible studio tools, or unauthorized redistributions as required dependencies. |
| Linguistic parity | Search Japanese and English literature on their own terms; retain original titles/keywords; add other languages where justified. |
| Proportionality | Choose the least complicated method satisfying the declared goal; add custom tooling only for a documented deficiency or recurring saving. |

## Reconstruction states

- R0 discovery: locate a target, technique, source or developer.
- R1 descriptive: explain the candidate mechanism, evidence and uncertainty.
- R2 operational: specify an executable recipe and prerequisites.
- R3 reproduced: create an original specimen and retain repeatable evidence.
- R4 generalized: reproduce with a materially different specimen or context.
- R5 institutionalized: integrate and maintain a reusable workflow/tool with validation and handoff.

Historical curiosities may stop at R1. A portfolio-critical method ordinarily aims for R4; R5 is justified by recurring use, not prestige. An item can also be rejected, deferred, or blocked; a failed experiment is evidence, not a promotion.

## Terminal conditions

A — epistemic sufficiency: explain the selected target properties with documented or clearly labeled reconstructed accounts. B — operational sufficiency: reproduce and vary them independently within stated resources. C — production sufficiency: selected methods interoperate in a coherent environment and support original work.

C is the provisional terminal condition for the core project, assessed only against a bounded target set agreed in advance. A/B can close individual inquiries. Before declaring C: document essential methods at R4 (or explicitly justify exceptions); perform a clean rerun and integrated demonstration, including at least one traceable original illustration-derived asset with an independently reusable specification and edit/export handoff; record gaps, dependency licenses, performance, resource costs and acceptance results. Nonessential methods may remain open. No endpoint requires exhaustive historical research or building a universal engine.

## Exclusions

No obligation to emulate an entire console, clone commercial games, use every software package, build custom editors by default, or claim national superiority. No claims of educational effectiveness without appropriate evaluation. No arbitrary freeze of technical or aesthetic standards before the comparative studies.
